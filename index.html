DarkAges Version Catalog

A historical catalog of DarkAges and its related titles and international editions, including Legends of Darkness (LoD), Yami and Malaysia’s Legends games. Explore client and patch versions, release history and verified downloads.

Titles

United States · Singapore

DarkAges

A fantasy role-playing world centered on Temuair, where Aislings explore lands of faeries and magic. Adventure grows alongside a society shaped by player-run politics, religion, storytelling and art.

Launched in 1999 by Nexon’s U.S. company under game director Dave Kennerly. The company became independent as KRU Interactive in 2005 and continues to operate DarkAges for an international player community. NexonAsia’s separate Singapore service ran from 2000 until June 2003.

South Korea · Singapore

Legends of Darkness / 어둠의 전설

A fantasy adventure set in Mysosia, where warriors, rogues, wizards, priests and martial artists develop complementary abilities through class changes and advancement.

Developed and published by Nexon, Legends of Darkness entered commercial service in South Korea on January 3, 1998 and remains active there. NexonAsia introduced an English-language Singapore beta in December 2002, followed by a planned commercial launch in February 2003; that service closed in June 2003.

Japan

Yami / 闇の伝説

A Japanese-language fantasy adventure in Mysosia, a world inspired by medieval Europe. Players choose professions, explore quests and rely on their companions’ different strengths.

Operated by Nexon Japan, this Japanese-language edition began beta testing on April 16, 2001 and entered commercial service on September 4, 2001. Its service ended on November 30, 2005.

Malaysia

Legends of Orphelias

An English-language fantasy MMORPG with its own lands, adventuring paths, skills and spells. Its mythology follows the mutual destruction of Chadul and Danaan, after which Aisling sparks are born into Orphelias. The game offered extensive player-versus-player combat and growing creature companions.

Operated by TIME dotNet in Malaysia, this English-language edition was in beta by 2001. Its server closed on May 31, 2002 as the publisher moved to Legends of Temuair.

Malaysia

Legends of Temuair

An English-language fantasy world of towns, forests, crypts and labyrinths, inspired by mythic Europe and Asian animation. Cooperation, trade, religion and politics form part of the adventure alongside spells, skills and crafting.

Available by 2002 through TIME dotNet in Malaysia as the successor to Legends of Orphelias. The publisher’s website advertised it as running through at least 2004; a confirmed closure date has not been recovered.

Verification Notes

File and package identity

SHA-256 identifies the original installer or patch file and appears below its download filename. Each link names the file it actually opens. For archive and disc-image downloads, the bracketed contained file is the one verified by the hash; the hash does not identify the containing package.

Mirror labels distinguish download locations. Identical original files share one verified identity even when their linked filenames differ. Repacked archives identify installed or extracted files; multipart volumes appear together and omit individual hashes. An absent hash leaves the original standalone installer identity unspecified.

Package comparisons include every extracted file: executables, data, text, configuration, music and supporting files. Matching one executable or a version label alone does not establish whole-client equality. Extraction paths and packaging comparisons appear in Research Notes.

Regional variants

The Singapore 3.51 distributions differ in embedded help text. Temuair and Orphelias-labeled 4.20 packages share core files but differ in configuration and music.

No complete client packages have been established as identical across confirmed country editions. Individual files can match across releases: Singapore and U.S. 3.51 share a DarkAges.dat variant; Korean 9.100 production and 9.570 Hades share Legend.dat.

Malaysian service chronology

  • The publisher’s May 28, 2002 announcement explicitly closed Legends of Opherlias on May 31, 2002, and called it the original Legends project. October 2001 original-Legends beta instructions establish that project’s 2001 testing period. The catalog retains the requested Orphelias spelling.
  • The named Temuair beta is documented by June 2002. Its official homepage continued to advertise “Up and Running” in October 2003 and April 2004. This contradicts the preservation history’s late-2002 closure claim.
  • The dates beside “Up and Running” change with each capture and are dynamic page timestamps. They establish when the publisher page advertised the service, rather than new client releases or independently tested game-server availability.
  • An exact final Temuair shutdown month is not authenticated. Buck’s unexplained May 14, 2004 date and later website errors do not establish a service-ending day.
  • The 2001 Legends download page does not clearly distinguish original Legends/Orphelias from the later Temuair name. A Temuair launch in 2001 remains a research question.
  • The inspected Malaysian publisher material uses English titles; no authenticated Malay title text is attested.

Singapore launch and closure milestones

  • The September 13, 2000 DarkAges news scheduled commercial launch for September 30, 2000. The announcement confirms that schedule and an operating service in 2000, rather than proving the completed commercial opening on that exact day.
  • For English LoD, NexonAsia announced client downloads for December 20, 2002 and server opening for December 24, 2002. A later FunPack notice confirmed the beta server was up. Client publication and server opening are separate milestones.
  • The January 23, 2003 LoD notice scheduled commercialization for February 5, 2003.
  • NexonAsia explicitly announced server shutdown at the end of June 2003 and refunds for sponsored accounts extending beyond June 30. June 2003 is a confirmed closure month; an explicit final-server-day statement remains unlocated.
